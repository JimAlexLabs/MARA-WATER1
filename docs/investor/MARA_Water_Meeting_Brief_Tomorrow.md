## A. Two ideas you must separate (say this first — people confuse them)

### 1. Company shares / directors = **who owns and who controls the company**
- The company has **1,000 shares**.
- Shares decide: who votes, who is residual owner of plant/vehicle/name if the business is sold or continues after July 2027.
- **A director does not have to own shares.** You can appoint Jim as Managing Director with control of operations even if some shareholders own more paper than they sit on the board — and you can appoint a second director who owns **zero** shares (or very few).  
  **Director ≠ shareholder.** Don’t let the room merge those debates.

### 2. Profit-sharing (OPPA) = **who gets monthly cash from trading this season**
- This is **not** “owning the whole company forever.”
- Cash partners (Derek, Diana) put in working capital for **Oct 2026 – Jul 2027**.
- Each month, after costs and your on-site fee, leftover **distributable profit** is split by **agreed percentages**.
- When the season ends (or renews), you renegotiate. Shares may stay; the **profit % table can change**.

**One sentence for the table:**

> “Shares decide who owns the company. Profit share decides who gets paid from this season’s trading. Derek and Diana are buying into **this season’s profits**, not taking the factory from the family.”
